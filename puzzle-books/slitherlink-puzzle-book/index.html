What is inside

One hundred loops, in order of difficulty.

The book holds one hundred Slitherlink puzzles and nothing else: 25 easy, 30 medium, 30 hard and 15 challenge, arranged so that difficulty rises through the book without a jump in the middle. The grids grow with it, from small ones at the front to the full sized challenge ones at the end.

In front of the puzzles are the rules with one worked example, so the book stands on its own without this page. At the back are the solutions to all one hundred, numbered to match.

You get two PDF files: one laid out for A4 and one for US Letter, 78 pages each, the same puzzles in both. Black and white, fonts embedded, vector line work, so the dots and clue numbers stay crisp and there is plenty of room to draw the loop by hand.

The rules

Three rules, printed in the front of the book.

  1. Draw one closed loop along the grid lines. The otters need a single river: no branches, no crossings, no second loop.
  2. A number in a patch counts its sides. It says how many of that patch's four sides the loop runs along, from 0 to 3. A patch with no number says nothing at all.
  3. Exactly one loop fits every number. A cross on a side is your own note that the side stays dry.

One solution

Hard to see, not hard to guess.

A puzzle book can say unique solution and mean that somebody checked the answer once. We mean something narrower. Each puzzle is built by a program that also solves it, and the solver is asked to find two solutions; the puzzle is kept only if it finds exactly one, and only if it reached that one by applying rules rather than by drawing a segment to see whether the loop closes later.

In a loop puzzle that guarantee is worth a lot, because a wrong segment can look right for a long time and only fail at the far corner. Here the deduction is always available somewhere on the page.

Two more things the program checks before the book is printed: no two puzzles are the same, and no two have the same answer. The program also re-derives every clue from the printed answer and compares it with the printed puzzle.

Questions

Before you pay.

What exactly do I get?

Two PDF files: one laid out for A4 and one for US Letter, 78 pages each. The puzzles are the same in both, only the page size differs. Both are black and white with the fonts embedded, there is no DRM and no account, and the files are yours to keep and print as often as you like.

Is Slitherlink the same as Fences or Loop the Loop?

Yes, those are other names for the same puzzle, along with Takegaki. You draw one closed loop on the grid lines and each number says how many of its cell's four sides the loop uses. Nothing in this book depends on which name you learned it under.

How do I print it?

On any home or office printer, in black and white. Choose the file that matches your paper and print at 100 percent or actual size rather than fit to page, and the grids come out at the size they were drawn, with enough space between the dots to draw a loop in pen.

How do you know every puzzle has one solution?

Because a program checked all one hundred. The solver is run with a limit of two solutions; if it finds a second one the puzzle is thrown away. It also has to reach the solution by rules alone, without trial and error. On top of that the program re-derives every clue from the printed answer and compares it with the printed puzzle.
