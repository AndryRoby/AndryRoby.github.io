Is your shop showing the EU legal guarantee notice?

Online shops that sell goods to consumers must show the EU’s harmonised legal guarantee notice prominently, in colour and unedited, once their country applies Art. 6(1)(l) and Art. 22a of Directive 2011/83/EU, as amended by Directive (EU) 2024/825.

Check my shop

Slovakia and Germany (Art. 246a EGBGB) have applied these rules since 27 September 2026; in Czechia the transposing law had not been promulgated by 22 October 2026. The design and text of the notice are fixed in Annex I of Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/1960.

The official wording: directive and harmonised notice

The text of the notice (English version)

In the Official Journal the notice is an image. We read the text from that image and from the Commission’s official files; both match line by line. Heading: “LEGAL GUARANTEE”.

Use the Commission’s official file, not this transcribed text. The files exist in all 24 official languages as PDF, PNG and SVG: Commission files and guidelines.

Who has to show the notice, and from when

  • Every trader selling goods to consumers (Art. 5(1)(e) and Art. 6(1)(l) of Directive 2011/83/EU). Recital 28 of Directive (EU) 2024/825 gives examples for physical shops and for online sale (recitals are not binding).
  • Goods only. For digital content and digital services the directive asks for a reminder of the legal guarantee (Art. 6(1)(lb)), not the harmonised notice.

Legal guarantee notice or GARAN label: two different things

Many guides mix them up. The notice covers the whole shop. The GARAN label belongs to a single product and applies only in this case:

Art. 8(2) of Directive 2011/83/EU lists the information shown directly before the order as points (a), (e), (la), (o) and (p). Point (la), the GARAN label, is there; point (l), the notice, is not. That is our reading of the text, not a court ruling.

The Commission guidelines of April 2026 are not binding. Their examples put a link text such as “Your legal guarantee rights” on the catalogue page, in the header or at checkout, with the notice appearing on the first click or on mouseover. They also say:

German practitioners are more cautious. The law firm IT-Recht Kanzlei recommends showing the notice directly as a visible graphic. Its argument: the regulation expressly allows nested display only for the GARAN label (Annex II and recital 14). The retailers’ association Händlerbund also advises caution until the Commission clarifies this. We found no court ruling on this by 22 October 2026.

Make the choice deliberately and note why. If our check finds only such a link, it never reports it as an error, only as “Check by hand”.

Common mistakes

  • A sentence of your own such as “2-year warranty” instead of the harmonised notice.
  • The notice cropped, recoloured, shrunk until it is illegible, or translated by the shop.
  • The black-and-white version online instead of the colour RGB file.
  • The GARAN label shown across the shop although no producer offers such a guarantee.
  • The platform setting left off after an update.
  • No clickable link to europa.eu/youreurope/guarantees, although the Commission recommends one.

How our check searches

The check looks for the main sentence of the notice in English, German, Slovak or Czech in visible text, in image alt text and in aria-label. It also reports the address printed under the QR code or a link to the Your Europe page on guarantees. In the result, the notice is called “Notice on the legal guarantee”.

  • Notice found: the text is on a page we read. We do not check design, colours or placement.
  • Check by hand: we did not find the text, but we found a link to Your Europe or the address under the QR code. A link may open the notice when clicked; check the page yourself.
  • Notice not found: neither the text nor such a link was on the pages we read. The notice may still be there, as an image without text, in the cart or at checkout.

Alt text with the main sentence does not change the notice, helps screen reader users and makes the notice visible to our check.

How to check by hand

  1. Open a product page as a customer: can you see the colour notice without searching?
  2. Put a product in the cart and go as far as checkout without ordering.
  3. Repeat on a phone: is the notice legible at the default size?
  4. Tap the link or scan the QR code: do you reach the Your Europe page on guarantees?
  5. Read your order confirmation email: does it contain the notice or a link to it?
  6. Compare the graphic with the official file: same colours, same text, nothing cut off.

Side note: the withdrawal function

Directive (EU) 2023/2673 required Member States to apply national measures on a withdrawal function for distance contracts concluded through an online interface from 19 June 2026; Slovakia and Germany do, while in Czechia the rule applies from 1 January 2027. Under Art. 11a(1) of Directive 2011/83/EU, the function “shall be labelled with the words ‘withdraw from contract here’ or an unambiguous corresponding formulation in an easily legible way”; our check shows its status in a separate line of the result.

Source: Art. 11a(1) of Directive 2011/83/EU, inserted by Directive (EU) 2023/2673

Frequently asked questions

Is the legal guarantee notice mandatory?

Yes, for traders selling goods to consumers, from the date set by national law; in Slovakia and Germany that date is 27 September 2026. The duty is in Art. 6(1)(l) and Art. 22a of Directive 2011/83/EU, and Annex I of Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/1960 fixes the design and text.

What is the difference between the notice and the GARAN label?

The notice is for the whole shop (Annex I). The GARAN label belongs to a single product and is shown only when the producer offers a commercial guarantee of durability at no extra cost, covering the entire good, for more than two years, and gives the trader that information (Annex II, Art. 6(1)(la)).

Is a link that opens the notice enough?

The directive only says “in a prominent manner”. The non-binding Commission guidelines show such examples, while the German law firm IT-Recht Kanzlei recommends a visible graphic and the retailers’ association Händlerbund advises caution. It has not been settled; decide deliberately.

Must the notice sit directly above the order button?

Art. 8(2) of Directive 2011/83/EU lists point (la), the GARAN label, among the information shown directly before the order, but not point (l), the notice. That is our reading of the text; for your own case, ask a lawyer.

Can I edit or translate the notice myself?

No. “None of the elements of the harmonised notice can be edited” (note 1 to Annex I). The Commission provides the file in all 24 official languages.

Why does the check say “Notice not found” when the notice is in my shop?

The notice is usually an image without text. The check does not read text in images and cannot see the cart or checkout.
