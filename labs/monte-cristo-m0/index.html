Monte Cristo Wait and Hope

Chapters of Alexandre Dumas’s novel, played as they are written. Every line anyone speaks is the book’s own, with its line number.

Printing the scene

About this prototype.

This page is not linked from anywhere. It is the first playable slice of a game made from The Count of Monte Cristo: chapter 18, where the abbé Faria shows Edmond Dantès the half-burnt will of Cardinal Spada, and chapter 69, where a man sent by the police questions the abbé Busoni and Lord Wilmore about the Count of Monte Cristo, not knowing that both of them are the count.

Every line a character says, and every line of narration, is word for word from the Project Gutenberg text of the novel (eBook #1184), shortened only by leaving words out, which the game marks with an ellipsis. The small number beside each line is where it stands in that text. What the book does not say, the colour of a coat, a gesture, where the window is, is our staging, and our notes for the game mark it as such.

The pictures are drawn in code in your browser and printed like a risograph: three inks, halftone dots, paper grain, the inks slightly out of register. The sounds are made in your browser too. Nothing is a photo, a recording or a generated image.

Controls

Keyboard and mouse: A and D or the arrows move, or click where to go. E, Space or a click goes on; holding E or the mouse button holds things. Hold F or the right button to look closely, hold S to listen. 1 to 9 choose, Tab or J opens the journal, Backspace shows earlier lines, Esc pauses.

Gamepad: left stick moves, A goes on and holds, LT looks, B listens, Y is the journal, Start pauses. Touch: tap to go on or to walk, hold to hold, and the eye and ear buttons under the picture. Every hold can be switched to a single press in the settings, and nothing needs quick fingers.
