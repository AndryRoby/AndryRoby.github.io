    Names are recognized by their capital letters, and other meanings of AI by your text’s own definition, such as “avian influenza (AI)”. A heading in title case or an unusual name can slip through or be held back by mistake, so read the kept list before you publish.

    What the order says

    Updated 30 September 2026.

    On 29 September 2026 the President signed the executive order “Inaugurating The Era Of Super Intelligence”. Three sections decide what this checker changes.

    Section 2(a): who uses the new terms, and where

    To the maximum extent permitted by law, executive departments and agencies (agencies) shall use “Super Intelligence” and “SI” in place of “Artificial Intelligence” and “AI” in official correspondence, public communications, websites, reports, policy documents, and other non-statutory documents within the executive branch.

    Section 2(b): earlier documents

    Nothing in this section requires the alteration of previously issued regulations, Presidential actions, contracts, grants, or other historical documents.

    Section 3(a): what SI means

    For purposes of this order, and except where otherwise provided by law, the terms “Super Intelligence” and “SI” mean the technologies and systems encompassed by the term “artificial intelligence” as defined in section 9401(3) of title 15, United States Code.

    What the order does not require

    • Rewriting earlier documents. Previously issued regulations, Presidential actions, contracts, grants and other historical documents can stay as they are (Section 2(b)). The checker holds back titles of earlier documents for your review.
    • Changing other meanings of AI. SI means AI the technology (Section 3(a)), so AI/AN for American Indian and Alaska Native, or avian influenza (AI), stay.
    • Changes by private companies or contractors. The duty in Section 2(a) is on executive departments and agencies, and the order does not extend it to companies, contractors or grantees. Its only deadline, 60 days, is for the Assistant to the President for Science and Technology to propose legislative language for a Federal definition.
    • Renaming statutes. The order covers non-statutory documents, so the title of a law such as the Advancing American AI Act stays as it was enacted.
    • Anything in the European Union. The AI Act, Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, still defines an “AI system” in Article 3(1).

    This tool does not give legal advice. It suggests wording. You decide what to change, and your agency's instructions come first.

    Sources: Executive order, 29 September 2026 · White House fact sheet · CNBC · MLex · Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 on EUR-Lex

    Questions

    Do private companies have to stop saying AI?

    The order directs executive departments and agencies (summarized in the White House fact sheet). It does not extend the duty to companies, contractors or grantees, and its only deadline, 60 days, is for a proposed Federal definition. If you write for an agency, follow its instructions.

    Do I have to change documents that were already issued?

    No. Section 2(b) of the order says: “Nothing in this section requires the alteration of previously issued regulations, Presidential actions, contracts, grants, or other historical documents.” The checker holds back titles of earlier documents for your review.

    Why are statute titles not changed?

    The order covers official correspondence, public communications, websites, reports, policy documents and other non-statutory documents. A law such as the National Artificial Intelligence Initiative Act of 2020 keeps the title it was enacted with, so the checker holds it back for your review.

    Does this apply in the European Union?

    No. EU law keeps the term “AI system”, defined in Article 3(1) of the AI Act, Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. For EU documents, keep the EU wording.

    Which spellings does the checker find, and which not?

    It finds Artificial Intelligence in any capitalization, A.I. with or without the last period, and AI as a whole word, including AIs, AI's and compounds such as AI-powered. It does not find lowercase ai, Ai, A. I. with a space, or words that only contain the letters, such as OpenAI, GenAI, Thai, Mail, AIDS, AIM and FAIR.

    Is my text uploaded or stored?

    No. The check runs in your browser, and your text is not sent anywhere or saved. Our self-hosted Umami counts page visits and button clicks, without cookies and never with your text.
