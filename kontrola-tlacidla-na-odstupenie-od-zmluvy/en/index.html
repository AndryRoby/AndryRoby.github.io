Does your online shop have the EU withdrawal button?

For distance contracts concluded through a website or app, Article 11a of Directive 2011/83/EU, inserted by Directive (EU) 2023/2673, requires a withdrawal function labelled “withdraw from contract here” or an unambiguous corresponding formulation, prominently displayed and available throughout the withdrawal period.

Check my shop

It leads to an online withdrawal statement that the consumer sends with a confirmation function labelled “confirm withdrawal” or an unambiguous corresponding formulation; the trader then sends an acknowledgement of receipt on a durable medium, such as an email, without undue delay.

Member States had to apply their rules from 19 June 2026, but the start date and the exact words come from national law: Germany and Slovakia since 19 June 2026, Austria since 1 October 2026, Czechia from 1 January 2027, and Ireland uses “cancel contract here”.

Last checked on 23 October 2026: German, Austrian, Irish and Maltese law and the documentation of 11 shop platforms. The directive in the Official Journal and the Slovak and Czech laws were read on 22 October 2026.

Check the withdrawal button in your shop

Enter your shop’s address. We read its public pages and show whether a link or button carries the directive’s wording, such as “withdraw from contract here” or “Vertrag widerrufen”, or another link about withdrawal. It usually takes under a minute.

The check looks for the label, not the whole process. It runs no JavaScript, cannot see the customer account, the order or emails and submits nothing, so it does not test the form, the confirmation step or the acknowledgement email. “Label not found” does not mean the function is missing, and “Label found” does not mean the shop complies.

We read only public pages and files of your shop, without signing in and with at most 14 requests per check: robots.txt, the home page, the public product export /products.json (Shopify has one), possible product pages (at most 3 are evaluated), the returns, shipping and contact pages, llms.txt and, if needed, the sitemap. Limit: 6 checks per hour per IP, counted by an IP hash, never by the shop address. No sign-up and no email address needed; page contents are not saved.

The EU wording: Article 11a of Directive 2011/83/EU

Directive (EU) 2023/2673 inserted Article 11a into the Consumer Rights Directive 2011/83/EU, under the heading “Exercise of the right of withdrawal from distance contracts concluded by the means of an online interface”. All five paragraphs, word for word:

The amending directive’s title is about financial services, but Article 11a covers distance contracts concluded online in general. Recital 37, which is not binding, says: “This obligation should apply not only to distance contracts for financial services, but to all distance contracts that are subject to the right of withdrawal under Directive 2011/83/EU.”

Who it applies to

  • Distance contracts concluded through an online interface, such as a website or an app (Art. 11a(1)). The article does not speak of contracts concluded by phone or in a shop.
  • Contracts with a right of withdrawal. Recital 37 speaks of distance contracts “subject to the right of withdrawal”. The exceptions to that right (Article 16 of Directive 2011/83/EU and national law) are not listed here.
  • An extra way, not a replacement. The consumer can “also” withdraw by using the function (Art. 11a(1)). The existing ways, such as the model withdrawal form, stay available (recital 37).

If you sell from outside the EU, or to consumers in several Member States, which national law applies to your contracts is a legal question that this page does not answer. The table above covers the six laws we have read.

What our check reports

The check looks for the directive’s two labels, “withdraw from contract here” and “confirm withdrawal”, in nine language versions: English, German, Slovak, Czech, Polish, French, Italian, Spanish and Dutch, and the Irish statutory labels “cancel contract here” and “confirm cancellation”. The words must come in this order; capitals and accents do not matter, and up to two other words may be added, so “Withdraw from the contract here” counts. It reads the text of links and buttons, including form buttons, and their aria-label and title. Labels about consent or cookies, such as “Withdraw consent”, never count.

How to add it on your platform

Only what each platform’s own documentation, release notes or source code says, read on 23 October 2026. Even where the function is built in, you often have to switch it on or add the link yourself. Where a platform has a default or suggested English label, we say what our check reports for it.

Shopify

No separate withdrawal button. Shopify’s help points to self-serve returns and cancellations in customer accounts (Settings > Customer accounts) and to return and cancellation rules (Settings > Policies); to send a request, the customer signs in with an email and a six-digit code. You add a visible link yourself (Content > Menus, Footer menu) with a label you choose; our check reads that label. Shopify writes: “Using these tools doesn't automatically make your store compliant.” Apps in the Shopify App Store also add withdrawal forms; we do not recommend one.

Source: help.shopify.com: EU right of withdrawal; help.shopify.com: setting up self-serve returns

WooCommerce

Built in since version 11.1.0 (3 September 2026) as “Order withdrawal”, off by default: WooCommerce > Settings > Advanced > Features. The form at /my-account/withdraw-order/ works without signing in, but “it is not linked anywhere by default”: add the link to the footer or the order emails yourself. Default labels: the page title “Withdraw from contract”, then the buttons “Continue to review” and “Confirm withdrawal”. The documentation adds that the feature “does not guarantee compliance”. A link labelled like the page title shows as “Check the link” in our check.

Source: woocommerce.com: Customer order withdrawal; developer.woocommerce.com: Order withdrawal in WooCommerce 11.1; github.com: WooCommerce 11.2.1 withdrawal form template

Shopware 6

Built in since version 6.7.9.0 (April 2026), in 6.6 since 6.6.10.14. Switch on the footer button under Settings > General > Basic Information, tab “Legal and compliance”. Default labels: “Revoke a contract” and “Confirm revocation” in English, “Vertrag widerrufen” and “Widerruf bestätigen” in German. With a custom theme or a headless frontend, Shopware says the merchant or the agency must build the visible button. Our check reports “Check the link” for “Revoke a contract” (a default text of the platform, not the wording of a law) and “Label found” for the German label.

Source: docs.shopware.com: Basic Information; shopware.com: withdrawal button requirement, 10 March 2026; github.com/shopware/shopware (default labels)

Not built in according to the official sources. The PrestaShop blog of 5 March 2026 points to modules on PrestaShop Marketplace, “including modules to help you add a withdrawal button on your site”. The label, sign-in and confirmation email depend on the module you choose.

BigCommerce

Not built in. The BigCommerce blog of 1 July 2026 says: “The company doesn't offer a native withdrawal function yet, so this is something to set up at the store level for now.” Its checklist suggests a “Withdraw from contract here” link visible on every page and a form that works without login for guest consumers; such a link gives “Label found” in our check.

Wix

Not built in. Wix’s help explains how to build it: a page with a form and a button that links to it, with the suggested labels “Cancel a Contract” and “Submit cancellation request”. Customers should be able to send a request without creating an account or logging in, and the confirmation is up to you: “Make sure your chosen solution provides this confirmation where required by local law.” Our check does not count “Cancel a Contract” as a withdrawal label.

Not built in. A button labelled “Withdraw contract” shows as “Check the link” in our check.

Built in, in Jimdo Creator and Jimdo Dolphin: “From 8 June 2026, Jimdo automatically enables the withdrawal button on EU websites.” In Creator the footer button, “Withdraw contract” in English, opens a form hosted by Jimdo at /j/withdrawal, and “No login is required for the consumer.” Jimdo does not store the requests (“Keep the notification emails as your record.”) and advises mentioning the button in your withdrawal policy. A request does not cancel or refund the order by itself. Ecwid’s article does not mention a link in the site footer. Common mistakes

  • A link only to the terms or to the model withdrawal form. The function must let the consumer send an online withdrawal statement (Art. 11a(2)). The model form stays available as an additional way (recital 37) but does not replace the function.
  • The function only after signing in. Article 11a does not mention logging in; it requires the function to be “easily accessible to the consumer”. Recital 37, which is not binding, says the consumer “should not have to undertake procedures to find or access the function, such as downloading an application if the contract was not concluded via that application”, and mentions logging in only for a consumer “who has already identified himself or herself, for example by logging in”, who should not have to identify again. A customer who ordered as a guest has no account. The explanatory memorandum of the German government bill says the function “muss grundsätzlich auch ohne Login erreichbar sein” (must in principle be reachable without login); that is not the text of the law.
  • A footer link that looks like every other legal link. The function must be “prominently displayed”. For a footer link, the same German memorandum asks for measures such as colour, contrast and a prominent placement that clearly set it apart from the terms and conditions or the legal notice.
  • No confirmation step, or no acknowledgement. The statement is sent with a separate confirmation function (Art. 11a(3)), and the trader sends an acknowledgement of receipt with its content and the date and time of submission, without undue delay (Art. 11a(4)).
  • A button that disappears after a few days. The function must be “continuously available throughout the withdrawal period” (Art. 11a(1)).
  • Withdrawal instructions that do not mention the function. Where applicable, the information on the right of withdrawal includes “the existence and placement of the withdrawal function” (Art. 6(1)(h)).
  • One date for the whole EU. 19 June 2026 is the date from which Member States had to apply their rules. Austria applies its rule to contracts concluded after 30 September 2026, Czechia from 1 January 2027.
  • A label about returns, such as “Return items”. It does not say withdrawal, and our check does not count it.

Source: Bundestag printed paper 21/1856, explanatory memorandum to § 356a BGB (German)

  • 2 of 4: Label found. Both had a link or button labelled “Vertrag widerrufen” on the home page. Whether it opens a working form, we did not test.
  • 1 of 4: Check the link. It had only links labelled “Widerrufsbelehrung” (withdrawal instructions), “Widerrufsformular” (withdrawal form) and “Widerruf” (withdrawal). Whether one of them opens an online form, the check cannot tell.
  • 1 of 4: Could not read. The check could not read any page of the shop.

How to check it yourself

  1. Open your shop in a private window without signing in, on a computer and on a phone, and look for the withdrawal link without using search, for example in the footer.
  2. Click it as a customer who ordered as a guest. Note whether it asks you to sign in.
  3. Fill in a name, the number of a test order and an email address. Check that nothing has been sent yet.
  4. Click the confirmation button: “Confirm withdrawal” or the wording of your national law.
  5. Open the email: it should contain the content of your statement and the date and time of submission (Art. 11a(4)).
  6. Repeat the test a few days after delivery: the function must work throughout the withdrawal period.
  7. Check that your withdrawal instructions say that the function exists and where it is (Art. 6(1)(h)).

If you sell goods, also check the EU legal guarantee notice, which Slovakia and Germany have required since 27 September 2026. The same withdrawal check also exists in German, Slovak and Czech.

Enforcement: what the laws allow

Penalties are set by each Member State. Directive 2011/83/EU requires effective, proportionate and dissuasive penalties and, where fines are imposed in a coordinated action under Article 21 of Regulation (EU) 2017/2394, a maximum of at least 4% of the trader’s annual turnover in the Member States concerned, or at least EUR 2 million where turnover information is not available (Article 24(1), (3) and (4)); it sets no other amounts. Below are the maximums written in the national laws we read, not typical fines. We found no published fine for this duty in the sources we read.

  • Slovakia: for breaches of § 20a(1) to (5), a fine from EUR 200 to 2% of the previous accounting period’s turnover, at most EUR 200,000 (§ 43(1)(a) of Act No. 108/2024 Coll.); higher ranges apply to repeated breaches (§ 43(3): from EUR 300 to 3%, at most EUR 400,000) and in coordinated actions against widespread infringements (§ 43(2): from EUR 500 to 4%). Supervision: the Slovak Trade Inspection, in some sectors another authority.
  • Czechia, from 1 January 2027: not enabling withdrawal by the button (§ 1830a(1)) or not confirming receipt (§ 1830a(5)) is an offence with a fine of up to CZK 5,000,000 (§ 24(16)(q) and (r) and § 24(24)(e) of Act No. 634/1992 Coll.); only in proceedings under Article 21 of Regulation (EU) 2017/2394, up to 4% of total annual turnover, or up to CZK 50,000,000 where turnover information is not available (§ 24(25)). Supervision: the Czech Trade Inspection Authority.
  • Germany: a fine of up to EUR 50,000, or up to 4% of annual turnover for traders with more than EUR 1.25 million, but only for widespread infringements and only within a coordinated enforcement action under Article 21 of Regulation (EU) 2017/2394 (Art. 246e § 1(1) and § 2(2), (3) and (5) EGBGB). Authority: the Federal Office of Justice.
  • Austria: an administrative offence with a fine of up to EUR 1,450, unless the act is a criminal offence or another administrative provision sets a stricter penalty (§ 19(1) no. 6a FAGG); up to 4% of annual turnover only in proceedings under Article 21 of Regulation (EU) 2017/2394 (§ 19(3) FAGG).
  • Ireland: a trader who contravenes section 115A(1), (2), (3) or (4) commits an offence (section 115A(6)). We have not verified the penalty.

Frequently asked questions

What exact words must the withdrawal button use?

The directive says “withdraw from contract here” or an unambiguous corresponding formulation, and for the confirmation step only “confirm withdrawal” or an unambiguous corresponding formulation (Art. 11a(1) and (3)). National laws use their own words, for example “Vertrag widerrufen” in Germany and Austria, “odstúpiť od zmluvy tu” in Slovakia, “Odstoupit od smlouvy” in Czechia from 1 January 2027 and “cancel contract here” in Ireland.

Is a downloadable form or an email address enough?

Not as a replacement. Article 11a(1) says the consumer “can also withdraw from the contract by using a withdrawal function”. The model withdrawal form and other ways stay available in addition (recital 37).

Can the button be only in the customer account?

Article 11a does not mention logging in; recital 37 mentions it only for a consumer who is already logged in and should not have to identify again. The article asks that the function be prominently displayed, easily accessible and continuously available throughout the withdrawal period, and a customer who ordered as a guest has no account. The explanatory memorandum of the German government bill says the function must in principle be reachable without login. Whether your solution is enough is a question for a lawyer; our check cannot see customer accounts.

From when does the withdrawal button apply in my country?

From the date in your national law. In the laws we read: Germany and Malta since 19 June 2026, Slovakia for contracts concluded online after 18 June 2026, Ireland since 30 June 2026, Austria for contracts concluded after 30 September 2026 and Czechia from 1 January 2027. For other Member States, check the national law.

Why does the check say “Label not found” when my shop has a button?

The check reads only some public pages, runs no JavaScript and cannot see the customer account or emails, so a link that appears only there, or in a menu built by a script, stays hidden. It also looks only for the directive’s words in nine languages and the Irish statutory words, so a label such as “Cancel a Contract” is not recognised; “Revoke a contract” gets “Check the link”. Check the function by hand.

Is the check free?

Yes. No sign-up and no email address; up to 6 checks per hour from one IP address. It reads public pages and files only and does not save their content.
