    What a page looks like

    A reading page, a running head and room for the pen.

    The picture is a real page of the e-ink file, not a mockup. The chapter opens with its number and title, the running head names the book you are in, and the bottom of the page carries the Contents link and the page number.

    The text block is 139 x 194 mm on a page of 157 x 210 mm, set in 13 pt serif with 22 lines to a page and no hyphenation, so a line break never falls inside a word. Italics are kept wherever the source transcription marks them.

    Under the text there is a 33 mm band left empty on every page. It is there for a pen: a note, a date, a name you want to remember. The band is also a PDF form field, so you can write in it with the pen on an e-ink tablet, or type into it on a computer in Adobe Acrobat Reader, Preview or Firefox. Under the band sit the Contents link and the page number, so from any page you are two taps from any chapter.

    The A4 and US Letter files hold the same text block in the middle of the sheet, so the pages, the page order and the links are the same in all three files.

    Where the words come from.

    The original English text of Ben-Hur: A Tale of the Christ, first published by Harper and Brothers, New York, in 1880. It is in the public domain: Lew Wallace died in 1905. The text comes from a public domain digital transcription and is reproduced unchanged, including the author's dedication and his three footnotes. Only the line breaks were reflowed for the page and italics restored where the transcription marks them.
