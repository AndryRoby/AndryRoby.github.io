First

What to know first

Power first

Without electricity there is no router, no computer and no offline AI on a computer. A phone lasts as long as its battery. So keep power banks charged: according to BBK, they let you charge a phone or laptop during an outage. Unplug appliances and electronics during the outage so a surge does not damage them when the power returns (Ready.gov).

Internet is not the same as a call

An outage might affect only certain networks, or all of them (UK government). When the network is overloaded, text messages need less bandwidth and get through more reliably than calls (Ready.gov). You cannot count on it.

Often hours, sometimes days

According to BBK, power outages are usually fixed within a few hours, but with widespread damage it can take days. In the total blackout in Spain on 28 April 2025, 99.95% of demand was restored by 7:00 the next day (Red Eléctrica).

Fire and gas indoors

In an outage people heat and cook with alternative sources. Carbon monoxide cannot be seen or smelled and can kill (CDC). Grills, camping stoves and generators do not belong indoors, and never leave candles burning unattended. Details are in Grills, stoves, generators and gas.

Checklist

A 72 hour checklist

The European Union aims for every household to manage on its own for at least 72 hours. For a blackout the Slovak Ministry of Interior recommends larger supplies; see the questions below.

  1. Water

    Water for everyone for three days. For drinking, count on at least 2 litres per person per day (72h.gov.cz); the Slovak Ministry of Interior recommends keeping 3 to 5 litres of drinking water per person per day, and for a blackout at least 4 litres (2 for drinking and 2 for cooking and hygiene). With hygiene and cooking, use reaches 10 to 15 litres a day according to 72h.gov.cz. Unopened bottled water is the safest (CDC), and 72h.gov.cz says to drink only bottled water or water from emergency tankers.

    If you store water yourself, never use containers that held chemicals such as bleach or pesticides (CDC). Before filling a container, wash it with soap and rinse it thoroughly. Then sanitize it with a solution that is only for the container, not for drinking: mix about 5 ml of unscented liquid household chlorine bleach containing 5 to 9% sodium hypochlorite into a litre of water (the amount is our conversion from US units). Pour the solution into the container, close it tightly and shake it well so the solution reaches every inside surface. After at least 30 seconds pour the solution out and let the empty container air-dry (CDC). If you are not sure about the water quality, boil it (Slovak Ministry of Interior).

  3. Medicines and first aid kit

    A first aid kit and the medicines you need for a week (72h.gov.cz). Slovak Ministry of Interior: first aid kit, bandages, important medicines and your prescribed medicines. If you take medicines that must be refrigerated or use a medical device that runs on electricity, agree a power outage plan with your doctor in advance (Ready.gov). According to the CDC, when the power is out for a day or more, throw away medicines that should be refrigerated unless the label says otherwise; if a life depends on such a medicine and it has been at room temperature, use it only until a new supply is available, and replace all refrigerated medicines as soon as possible. Check expiry dates and keep medicines out of children's reach (Slovak Ministry of Interior). Learn first aid on a Slovak Red Cross course (in Slovak); this directory is no substitute.

  11. Warmth without heating

    Warm clothes and blankets, a sleeping bag or warm blanket for everyone. According to BBK, stay in one room if possible and keep the doors closed so warmth does not escape, but still ventilate regularly, especially if you use candles. The Slovak Ministry of Interior also says to air the room regularly. If the heat or cold is extreme, go to a community location with power (Ready.gov).

    Do not heat a flat with a gas stove, a grill or a generator, and use a gas heater only with its safety devices. Do not burn anything in a stove or fireplace, or cook on it, if it is not vented (CDC).

    Under Slovak Ministry of Interior Decree No. 401/2007 Coll., a chimney connected to solid or liquid fuel appliances with a total heat output of up to 50 kW must be cleaned and inspected at least once every four months while in use. If the chimney has not been in use for longer than those four months since its last cleaning and inspection, it must be inspected and cleaned before you light the appliance again. A blocked chimney can let carbon monoxide build up indoors (CDC). More on carbon monoxide in Grills, stoves, generators and gas.

Safety and pitfalls

Safety and what not to rely on

Grills, stoves, generators and gas: carbon monoxide indoors

Carbon monoxide is an odourless, colourless gas that can cause sudden illness and death (CDC). The most common symptoms of poisoning are headache, dizziness, weakness, nausea, vomiting, chest pain and confusion; people who are asleep can die before they have any symptoms. If someone shows symptoms, the CDC says to call emergency services or a health professional right away; in Slovakia that is 112 or the ambulance on 155 (Slovak Ministry of Interior).

Never heat a home with a gas range or oven. Never run a generator or any petrol engine inside a home, basement or garage, even with doors and windows open; use it only outside, more than 20 feet (about 6 m) from windows, doors and vents (CDC). The German BBK: do not grill in a flat or house.

The Slovak Ministry of Interior guide lists gas or paraffin heaters as alternative heat sources, but we found no carbon monoxide warning there. Use a gas heater only if it has an oxygen depletion safety device and a flame failure device, and add a carbon monoxide detector (BBK). A paraffin (kerosene) heater belongs in a well ventilated room, away from curtains and anything flammable; never overfill it, and the heater should have a thermostat and switch off by itself if it falls over (Ready.gov). Use only the fuel its maker specifies and follow the maker's instructions (CDC); the US site Ready.gov names crystal clear K-1 kerosene. Never burn charcoal indoors and do not use portable flameless chemical heaters indoors (CDC).

The fridge does not last long

According to the CDC, food stays safe in a fridge without power for 4 hours, frozen food 48 hours in a full freezer and 24 hours in a half full one. Keep the doors closed. After 4 hours without power or another cold source, throw out meat, fish, cut fruit and vegetables, eggs, milk and leftovers from the fridge. Never taste food to check whether it is safe; when in doubt, throw it out. Slovak Ministry of Interior: use food from the fridge first.

Frequently asked questions

Do internet and mobile work during a power outage?

Not necessarily. The German civil protection office says internet and the mobile network can be disrupted in a power outage (BBK). The UK government adds that an outage might affect only certain networks, or all of them (Be Ready).

When the network is overloaded, text messages get through more reliably than calls (Ready.gov). According to BBK, radio keeps broadcasting in an outage, so keep a battery or crank radio.

How much water and food should I keep at home?

At least 2 litres of drinking water per person per day. The Slovak Ministry of Interior recommends keeping 3 to 5 litres of drinking water per person per day, and for a blackout at least 4 litres (2 for drinking and 2 for cooking and hygiene) and food for the whole household for at least 14 days. The Czech 72h.gov.cz says food for at least 72 hours, and the German BBK supplies for ten days if possible.

The EU goal is self-sufficiency for at least 72 hours (European Commission). Our checklist is built around those 72 hours.

Do I need a licence for walkie talkies or Meshtastic?

Not for PMR446 walkie talkies: in Slovakia anyone may use them under general authorisation VPR-08/2022, at no more than 500 mW and only as handheld units with an integrated antenna.

LoRa radios (Meshtastic, MeshCore) in the 869.4 to 869.650 MHz band need no individual licence either: general authorisation VPR-02/2022 covers everyone in Slovakia, at no more than 500 mW e.r.p.; in the EU Meshtastic also keeps to a 10% duty cycle (Meshtastic). An amateur radio station, by contrast, needs an individual licence after an exam.
