Two Stars.

Two stars in every row, every column and every outlined region. No two stars may touch, not even at a corner.

A new puzzle every day at midnight. Every puzzle has exactly one solution.

Rules.

Three sentences. The rest is practice.

How to play

Place exactly two stars in every row, every column and every outlined region. The grid has nine rows, nine columns and nine regions, so there are eighteen stars in total.

No two stars may touch: not side by side, not diagonally.

A dot is your own note that a cell stays empty. The game does not check dots. When you place the last star, the game checks the solution by itself.

Controls

Tap or click a cell: once for a dot, twice for a star, three times to clear it. Hold the button or your finger and drag to sweep dots over many cells at once. A star that breaks a rule turns red. Check tells you how many of your stars are wrong, never which ones.

Keyboard: arrows move, Space or Enter cycles the cell, X dot, S star, Delete or Backspace clears. Undo takes back the last move, Clear empties the whole grid.

The clock runs from your first move to the last star. Your progress, time and day streak stay in this browser.

A few ways in

A region that fits inside one row or one column holds both stars of that row or column, so every other cell in that line is empty.

Two regions that together cover exactly two rows use up all four stars of those rows. Any cell of a third region inside those rows is empty.

A star empties all eight cells around it. When a region has only a few cells left, count how many stars still fit without touching.

About this game.

The puzzle is generated right in your browser from today's date: new every day and the same for everyone. The program first solves it itself and only accepts a puzzle with exactly one solution. No puzzle is taken from anywhere else. The mechanic itself is a classic pencil puzzle known under several names; this implementation, the grids and the words are ours.

Your grid, time and day streak are stored only in this browser. Our anonymous statistics (self-hosted Umami) record the page visit, as on every page of ours, and one extra event: that someone solved the puzzle and in how many seconds. Nothing else.
