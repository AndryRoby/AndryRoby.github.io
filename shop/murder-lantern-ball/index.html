Murder at the Lantern Ball.

Halloween, 1934. At two minutes to midnight the owner of the Grand Marlowe Hotel is found poisoned in the quiet Winter Garden, while 5,000 guests in costume dance in the ballroom. Nobody has left the building. You get sixteen true facts about the killer and the hotel register of every guest. Strike out the guests clue by clue until one name is left.

How you solve it.

One rule at a time, with a pencil. The film shows the real count from the case, from 5,000 guests down to one.

  1. Read a clue. For example: “Table is less than 24.” Every clue is true for the killer.
  2. Strike out every guest it rules out. The register is sorted by surname, like a phone book, so a clue about the first letter lets you skip whole pages.
  3. Write down the count. After clues 4, 8 and 12, compare it with the checkpoint. A different number? Go over the last four clues again.
  4. One guest is left. That is the killer. Check your answer on the last page.

Before you buy.

How does an elimination mystery work?

You read one clue at a time, for example “Table is less than 24”, and strike out every guest in the register for whom it is false. You write down how many guests are left. After clues 4, 8 and 12 a checkpoint tells you how many should be left, so you know you are on track. When one guest is left, you have found the killer.

Page 3 explains how to read each kind of clue (“more than 30” means 31 or more, times use the 24 hour clock) and works through an example.

Is there really only one answer?

Yes. A computer program built the case, and two separate programs, written in two different programming languages, checked the printed clues against the printed register: exactly one guest fits all sixteen clues, every clue removes at least 5 percent of the guests still in play, and every checkpoint number is right. Each clue can be checked on a single row; no outside knowledge and no guessing.

Is the solution included?

Yes, on the last page of every file: the killer, how many guests are left after every clue, and each clue checked against the killer’s row. Nothing on this page or in the film gives the killer away.

Can I solve it on a tablet instead of printing?

Yes. The E-ink file is 157 x 210 mm, sized for e-ink tablets, with tap to jump letters through the register. You can also open the A4 file on any tablet and mark it up in a PDF app. Our program checked the links in the E-ink file; we have not tried it on every device, so if something does not work on yours, write to us.

How long does it take?

We have not timed it with players, so we do not promise a time. Expect a long, careful puzzle rather than a quick one: the first clues cut through thousands of guests, the last ones through a handful.

Are the guests real people?

No. It is a work of fiction. A computer program combined the names at random from public lists of common U.S. first names and surnames; any match with a real person is chance.
