The Case of the Muddy Footprints.

A first detective game you print at home. Muddy footprints lead across the clean library floor at Acorn Hill School, and The Big Book of Pond Life is gone from the shelf. Four friends had break at the same time, and only one of them was in the library at 10:40. Your young detectives hunt for six picture clues, crack a picture code and cross out places on a big chart until only one friend is left.

What you get

Two PDF files of the same 15 pages: one A4 with British spelling and one US Letter with American words (sweater, sandbox, recess). You print them at home, cut out the cards and play at a party, in class or on a rainy afternoon. Nothing is shipped.

How the detectives play.

Four steps. A grown-up reads the case file aloud and hides the cards; the children do the rest.

  1. Hunt for the evidence. Six cards hide around the room, and each one rules somebody out of a place.
  2. Compare the pictures. Whose feet are webbed? Who is too big for the sticky door? Who wears yellow? The friend cards and the footprint guide have the answers.
  3. Crack Button’s code. Warm-up words first, then the picture code key turns Button’s secret note into words.
  4. The reveal. Read the top secret letter aloud and everyone goes to find the book. Before you play, you tape the book label onto any book at home and hide it where the letter says.

Before you buy.

What age is it for?

Children aged 6 to 9. For 6 and 7 year olds, put one child who can read in each team, or let a grown-up read the cards aloud. 8 and 9 year olds can play on their own, with an optional hard mode.

To be honest, this is a first mystery: a 9 year old who already likes logic puzzles may find it easy, even in the hard mode. For children of 8 to 12 there is a bigger case with five suspects and eight clues, The Case of the Missing Birthday Cake.

How many children can play, and how long does it take?

2 to 8 players, or 1 child with a grown-up. With 5 or more children, play in teams of 2 to 4. The game takes 30 to 45 minutes with the evidence hunt and about 20 minutes without it, plus about 20 minutes to print, cut and hide the cards, or about 10 minutes with the no scissors pages.

Has it been played with real children?

Not yet, and we would rather say so. Every page was checked at print size by our own check script, which measures every page and makes sure no text runs off a card or the page, and the chart was checked by a computer solver.
