A Christmas escape room, one note a day.

Grandpa Tobias the hedgehog forgot where he put the golden star. His 24 notes, one a day from December 1 to 24, are puzzles, and every answer gives one letter of where the star is waiting.

  • A new puzzle every day. Nine kinds: picture code, mazes, counting, picture sudoku, logic puzzles, story sums, number steps, word search and the lock.

How it plays.

The children open one note a day. The Star Map on the table fills up letter by letter until December 24.

  1. Open today’s note. Solve the puzzle on it. A grown-up can read it aloud for younger children.
  2. Find the answer on the Star Board. Write the letter next to it in today’s box on the Star Map.
  3. On a bell day, keep the number. On December 3, 6, 11 and 14 the answer is a number; it also goes into a bell box on the Star Map.
  4. On December 24, open the lock. The four bell numbers open the little door in the clock tower, and the last note tells the end of the story.

A wrong answer is often not on the Star Board, or it gives a letter that does not fit the sentence, so the sentence helps children spot some mistakes themselves. It cannot catch every one: the solutions pages explain every answer for the grown-up.

A gentle story to read aloud.

Nobody is bad, nothing is stolen, and there is no countdown clock. A forgotten star, snow, cookies and singing.

Every year on Christmas Eve, the animals of Holly Hill put a big golden star on top of the village tree. Grandpa Tobias the hedgehog looks after the old clock tower, and he looks after the star too. In November he put it somewhere safe, so the winter wind could not blow it away, and then he could not remember where. Luckily he writes little notes.

The children are the Star Helpers. Along the way they meet Mila the mouse, Pip the robin, Finn the rabbit and Lotte the lamb, who turn up in the puzzles. The story page is page 3; the end of the story is a folded note of its own, opened only after the lock on December 24.

December 24: the door in the tower.

With the last letter the Star Map is full. The children read the whole sentence aloud, then open the lock.

The lock
Four numbers: the bell numbers from December 3, 6, 11 and 14, in that order.
The last note
The end of the story, folded like the others, opened when the children have the code.
A surprise, if you like
The last note says Grandpa Tobias left a little surprise and asks the children to ask you where to look. It can be anything small; if you do not want one, say that the surprise is the star back on the tree.
Certificates
Four Official Star Helper certificates to a page, to cut out and sign.

Print, fold, play.

About 20 minutes once to print and fold, by our estimate. After that, nothing to prepare each evening.

  1. Print all 36 pages. One sided, at 100 percent scale. Color is nice but not needed; all pages work in black and white too.
  2. Fold pages 7 to 31 once. Only the strip with the day number shows; a paper clip keeps a note closed.
  3. Keep the 25 folded notes in order. In a box, a bag or the pockets of an advent calendar, out of sight.
  4. Put out the Star Map and the Star Board. On the table or the fridge for the whole month. Keep the hints and solutions away from the children.

Who it is for, and who it is not for.

A good pick if

  • you have children aged 6 to 10 who like puzzles, and you want an advent calendar without screens
  • 1 to 4 siblings will solve together, or you teach a class (print a Star Map for each group)
  • you want a calm story with no villain and no countdown clock
  • you would rather print and fold once before December 1 than prepare something every evening

Not the right pick if

  • you want your children’s names on every card; that is a different product, our personalized advent treasure hunt
  • you want a printed calendar shipped to you; this is a printable PDF, and nothing is shipped
  • your children are younger than 6, or you want puzzles for teens and adults
  • you want a spooky or timed escape room; this one is gentle on purpose
  • you need another language; the text is in English
  • you want a game that families have already played and rated; nobody has played it yet, and the ages and times are our estimate

You need a printer, A4 or US Letter paper, a pencil, a box or bag for the folded notes, and a small surprise for December 24 if you like.

Before you buy.

When and where do I get my files?

After Etsy confirms your payment, download the two PDF files from Purchases and reviews on Etsy.com in a web browser; downloads are not available in the Etsy app. If you bought as a guest, use the download link in your Etsy receipt email. Nothing is shipped.

Can a 6 year old play it?

December 1 to 8 work from age 6 with a grown-up reading aloud. December 9 to 16 are a little harder, and December 17 to 24 are for ages 8 to 10 or younger children with help. Every note has a hint to read aloud, and hints are always allowed. Ages and times are our estimate: nobody has played the game yet.

What if the children get an answer wrong?

A wrong answer is often not on the Star Board, or it gives a letter that does not fit the sentence, so the sentence helps children spot some mistakes themselves. It cannot catch every one. The solutions pages explain every answer for the grown-up.

We missed a day. What now?

Do two notes the next day. The order of the letters only matters on the Star Map.

Can I use it with a class or several children?

Yes. 1 to 4 siblings can solve together with one Star Map. For a class, print a Star Map for each group; the notes can be read aloud to everyone. You may print as many copies as your own family, classroom or club needs; please do not share or resell the files.

Do I need a color printer?

No. All pages work in black and white too. Print one sided at 100 percent scale.
