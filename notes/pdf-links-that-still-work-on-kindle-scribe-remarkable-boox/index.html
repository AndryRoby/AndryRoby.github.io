Getting a PDF onto a Kindle Scribe, a reMarkable or a Boox so that the links still work

ARLing s. r. o. · 18 September 2026

A hyperlinked PDF is sold on the promise that you tap a number in the contents and land on that page. The tapping is the part nobody can test for you, because it happens on glass you own and we do not. What we can do is say exactly what travels inside the file, what the file does to stay out of the way of your reader, and what we have measured rather than hoped. The trip itself is dull: the file is copied to the device the same way any other book is, with the reMarkable application, with Send to Kindle for the Scribe, or over a cable into the device folder on a Boox, with nothing installed and no account of ours anywhere. What follows is about the interesting half: why a link inside a PDF is a real thing, where taps go missing, and which four numbers tell you whether a file was built with care.

How the file gets to the reader

You download the PDF on a computer or a phone and copy it across exactly as you would any other book: the reMarkable desktop or mobile application, Send to Kindle for the Scribe, or a cable and the device folder for a Boox. Nothing is installed and no account of ours is needed.

Two things are worth knowing before you start. Keep the PDF a PDF. A file converted into a reader's own book format is a different file, and the links you paid for were built as PDF links. And check the page size before you copy: a page of 157 by 210 millimetres is the 3:4 shape of a 10.3 inch screen and fills it, which is why our e-ink files use it.

Why a link inside a PDF is a real thing

Our books are written as ordinary web pages with ordinary links, and Chrome keeps them when it prints to PDF, as link annotations with a destination inside the same file. An annotation is a rectangle on a page with a destination attached, so it travels with the file to any reader that acts on it.

A book therefore has a shape you can navigate. In a puzzle book the contents page opens a puzzle, every puzzle page carries a link to its own solution and a link back to the contents, and every solution links back to its puzzle. In a reading book the same idea gives 592 links and 71 destinations inside one file.

Where a tap goes missing

The failure most people meet is not a broken link, it is a link the device never sees because it sits where the reader has reserved the glass for itself. An article on the subject, dated 29 July 2026, reports that after a firmware update the Kindle Scribe does not fire links near the top and the right edge and that a margin of about an inch helped, that a reMarkable takes roughly the outer 120 dots for its tool bar, which is 13.5 millimetres at 226 dots per inch, along with the bottom edge for quick browsing, and that Boox keeps the corners for page turns.

So we keep the navigation out of those bands: the buttons sit in a centred bar at the bottom, at least 14 millimetres from the left, 26 from the right, 15 from the bottom and 25.4 from the top edge of the glass. In the reading book the same bar measures at least 29 millimetres from the left and right edges and 15 from the bottom, and the links are meant for a finger, not the pen.

Four numbers worth asking about

Ask a seller for the numbers their own check produced, or measure them yourself. Dead links: our planner reports 4117 internal links, 1211 destinations, 0 dead and 0 links leaving the document. Links that leave the document matter more than they sound, because a link to the internet is a link that does nothing on a device with the radio off.

Then the shape of the file. A puzzle issue of 145 pages carries 598 links and 213 destinations with no page overflowing its sheet. And the size of the target: in the reading book the contents page alone carries 36 of the links, so the page you most want to tap is the page that was designed to be tapped.

What we have not tested

No file of ours has been opened on a physical reMarkable, Kindle Scribe, Boox or Supernote. The margins are measured in the PDF only, readers differ in how they support internal links and in which edge zones they keep for gestures, and the article above does not claim that a centred bottom bar is reliable on any particular device. Nothing here is a measured property of a device, and no Puzzle Post file has been opened on one either.

That is why we give a sample away: days 1 to 7 of the reading book are a separate PDF of 52 pages with 139 links and 0 dead, the same page size and the same kind of links as the book. Copy the sample across first and tap it. If a link does not respond on your device, you know before you pay.

Frequently asked questions

Does converting a PDF for my reader keep the links?

We have not tested that, and we would not rely on it. The links you bought exist as PDF link annotations inside the file, so the safe route is to keep the file a PDF and open it as one.

Why are the buttons at the bottom and not in the corner?

Because the corners and the outer edges are where readers reserve the glass for their own gestures, according to the article on the subject, so our navigation stays in a centred bar at the bottom, away from those bands. It is our decision about the geometry of the file, not a measurement of your device.

How do I know a file's links were checked at all?

By the numbers from the check. Ours are published: 0 dead links and 0 links leaving the document in the planner, 592 links and 71 destinations in the reading book.
