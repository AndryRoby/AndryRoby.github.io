PDF form fields: why you can type on a computer and the pen never asks

ARLing s. r. o. · 18 September 2026

A journal PDF and a puzzle PDF have the same quiet problem. On an e-ink tablet you take the pen and write on the page, and it works, because the pen leaves ink on top of the drawing and does not care what is underneath. Open the same file on a computer and there is nothing to write with. You click on a ruled line, you type, and nothing happens, because a PDF page is a drawing and a drawing does not accept letters. The part that accepts letters is a form field: a box declared in the file, invisible until you click into it, that holds what you type. This note says what a field is, how ours are put into an already finished file without changing how it looks, which puzzle types can honestly have one in every cell, and how to find out in one click whether a file you are looking at has any at all.

What a form field actually is

A form field is a box you can type into with a keyboard. Our files get theirs after the PDF is finished: a script walks the printed file and adds a one line text field over every grey writing line, and an empty field with no border and no fill draws nothing, so the file looks exactly as it did before.

Finding the lines is the fussy part. A line counts as a writing line only if all five conditions hold at once: it is a horizontal line or a thin filled rectangle, its grey is near the value 0.541 in each channel, the stroke is at most 0.8 of a point thick, it is at least 20 millimetres long, and it does not overlap a link annotation on the same page. That last condition is what keeps a field from landing on top of a tappable row in a contents page.

Why the pen does not need one

The reason is written at the top of the script: on an e-ink tablet you write with the pen directly along the line, while in Adobe Acrobat Reader, in Preview or in Firefox you can only write into a form field. So the field is not there for the tablet, it is there for the evening when the tablet is on the shelf and the file is open on a laptop.

The numbers in our files follow from that. Every writing line in the reading book is a real field, 1038 of them on 118 pages in the book and 229 on 26 pages in the sample, the same in all three formats. In the novels it is one field on every text page, 935 in Ben-Hur and 2257 across the five volumes of the other title.

Which grids can honestly have a field in every cell

In a puzzle book, a field in every cell would be a lie for some puzzle types, so not every type gets one. A field inside a grid cell goes to Killer Sudoku, Anti-knight Sudoku, Kakuro, Nonograms, Star Battle, Nurikabe and Numberlink, because in those the answer is the state of a single cell: a digit, a mark for a filled cell, a star or a path number.

Masyu gets no cell fields, because its answer is a loop along the edges between cell centres, and Slitherlink gets none either, because its answer sits on the edges between the dots and the grid of cells is not even drawn. Solution pages get no fields at all. In one monthly issue that works out at 4457 fields, of which 4157 sit in grid cells and 300 over the ruled notes lines, with the number of links unchanged at 598 before and after.

How to tell whether a file has any

You do not need a tool. Open the PDF in Adobe Acrobat Reader, in Preview or in Firefox and click on a line or into a cell. If a cursor appears and you can type, the field is there; if nothing happens, the file is only a drawing and you cannot write into it on a computer.

There is a second question worth asking a seller: whether adding the fields changed the file. Ours is answered by the script itself, which fails the build unless the number of link annotations on every page is the same before and after, every page rendered at 72 dots per inch is identical dot by dot before and after, and every page where a line or a grid was found came out with at least one field. In the reading book that check reports 592 links before and after, and identical pixels.

What we have not tested

Fields are measured in the file, not on a device. No file of ours has been opened on a physical reMarkable, Kindle Scribe, Boox or Supernote, and readers differ in what they do with form fields and with links. Whether the grey ruled lines are visible enough at 226 dots per inch is also untested.

If you plan to type rather than write, try the behaviour on your own reader first, with a free sample rather than a purchase. The samples carry the same fields as the books they come from, 229 of them in the reading sample.

Frequently asked questions

Do the fields change how the page looks?

No. An empty field has no border and no fill, so it draws nothing, and the check confirms that every page renders identically before and after the fields are added.

Can I write with a pen and type on the same page?

On the tablet the pen writes on the page as it always did; the field is what makes the same line usable from a keyboard in Adobe Acrobat Reader, Preview or Firefox.

Why does my Masyu page have no cell boxes?

Because its answer is a loop along the edges between cell centres, not a mark inside a cell, so a box in the cell would be misleading; Slitherlink is left out for the same reason.
