Why an A4 PDF does not fill an e-ink screen, with the arithmetic

ARLing s. r. o. · 18 September 2026

Open a printable puzzle book or a printable planner on a reMarkable or a Kindle Scribe and it looks wrong in a way that is hard to name: there is a white band down each side, the type is smaller than it should be, and the page feels further away than a page of a real book. Nothing is broken. The file was set for a sheet of paper, the screen is not that shape, and the reader is doing the only sensible thing with a page that does not fit, which is to shrink it until it does. This note does the arithmetic, because once you have seen the numbers you can tell from a product listing whether a file was made for your device or for a printer. It also says what else changes in a page built for glass, and when the paper file is still the one you want.

What the glass actually measures

A reMarkable 2 and a reMarkable Paper Pro both have 1404 by 1872 dots, and at 226 dots per inch that is 157.8 by 210.4 millimetres. A Kindle Scribe has 1860 by 2480 dots, and at 300 dots per inch that is 157.5 by 210.0 millimetres. Both come out at a ratio of 1.333, which is 3:4.

That is the number to hold on to. Our e-ink page is 157 by 210 millimetres, a ratio of 1.338, so it fills the display instead of floating in the middle of it. Boox readers of that size share the same 3:4 shape, which is why one page size serves several devices.

What happens to an A4 page on that screen

A4 is 210 by 297 millimetres, a ratio of 1.414, and US Letter is 215.9 by 279.4 millimetres, a ratio of 1.294. Neither is 3:4, so neither fits.

Take the common case. A reader showing a whole A4 page on a screen of 1404 by 1872 dots fits it by height and scales it to 0.708 of its size. The drawing ends up 148.8 millimetres wide, 9.0 millimetres of width stay empty, that is 4.5 millimetres down each side, and everything on the page is drawn at 71 percent of its size, so text set at 13 points arrives as 9.2 points. The band at the side is the visible half of the problem, and the type size is the half you feel after a while.

What else changes when a page is set for glass

Page size is the start, not the end. An e-ink page has no binding and nothing is trimmed, so the margin is 8 millimetres on all four sides and does not alternate; there is one puzzle to a page even on a small grid; the cell ceiling goes up from 22 to 30 millimetres because you write into it with a stylus; there are four solutions to a page instead of six; there are no page numbers, because the reader does its own paging; and the contrast is stronger, because the display has sixteen shades of grey.

Numbers can be measured after the fact rather than promised. In our planner the smallest type is 8 points, the smallest link is 2.9 by 3.4 millimetres, a day cell on a month page is 18 by 11 millimetres, and no page overflows. In the novels the text is 13 points on an 18 point line, 22 lines to a page, with nothing below 10 points anywhere in the file.

When the paper file is the one you want

There is no argument against A4. There is only an argument against using an A4 file on a screen that is not A4 shaped. So the same content ships as both: A4 is 210 by 297 millimetres, US Letter 215.9 by 279.4 millimetres, which makes US Letter 5.9 millimetres wider and 17.6 millimetres shorter, and both sheets carry the same block, so nobody has to change the scale in a print dialogue.

In the reading books that block is 139 by 194 millimetres, centred on the sheet with a page number under it, so the pages, their order and the links are identical in all three files. Print at 100 percent rather than scaled to fit, and what comes out of the printer is what was measured.

Frequently asked questions

Is 157 by 210 millimetres a standard paper size?

No, it is a screen size. It is the 3:4 shape of a 10.3 inch e-ink display, which is why a page of that size fills the display without bars at the sides. For paper we use A4 and US Letter instead.

Will an e-ink file print properly?

It will print, but it is the wrong file for the job: it has an 8 millimetre margin on every side with nothing for a binding, and no page numbers. That is why the print formats exist as separate files with their own margins.

Does a bigger screen change the answer?

The ratio is what matters, not the diagonal. The reMarkable panels, the Kindle Scribe panel and Boox readers of that size all come out at 3:4, so one page shape covers them. A device with a different ratio needs a different layout, and we have not built one.
